Assignment: Why My Cricket Team Keeps Losing the Second Innings

We have lost eleven of our last fourteen matches in the colony league, and in
nine of those defeats we were ahead at the halfway mark. That pattern seemed
too consistent to be bad luck, so I kept a notebook for the whole season and
wrote down what happened in every over after the break.

The clearest finding is that our bowling changes are reactive. Our captain
waits for a batsman to hit two boundaries before switching ends, by which
point the batsman has settled and the field is already spread. In the four
matches where a change came before the scoring rate climbed, we conceded
fourteen fewer runs on average in the middle overs.

Fielding was the second problem, though not in the way I expected. We drop
very few catches. What we lose is the single that becomes a two because nobody
backs up the throw. I counted thirty one overthrows across the season, which
is roughly three matches' worth of margin in a league where most games finish
within ten runs.

The third factor is simply heat. Our home matches start at two in the
afternoon, and our opening bowlers are also our top order, so they bat for an
hour and then bowl without a proper break. The two games we played in the
morning slot were both wins, which is far too small a sample to prove
anything, but it is the only variable we can change without recruiting anyone.

My suggestion to the team is unglamorous: rotate the bowling every three overs
regardless of what the scoreboard says, assign a permanent backup fielder for
every throw, and request the morning slot whenever the ground is free. None of
this requires better players. It requires deciding these things before the
match instead of during it.
